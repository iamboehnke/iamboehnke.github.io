Job search

My own job search, charted from my records: how many applications were ignored, where the rest dropped out of the funnel, and which sectors, places and roles answered at all.

Started May 2026

Python, pandas, Plotly.js, PapaParse, Gmail API, HTML/CSS

What it is

— applications, — months and still going. A data visualization of my own job search - ghosting rates, funnel drop-off, and response rates by sector and location. I used my email archive and my own records to track how many organisations responded, how many sent a personal reply, and how far each application got. It is all rendered from a CSV file and updated whenever I add new outcomes. Of the applications old enough to judge, — were ghosted.

The funnel

Of all applications, the vast majority either received no reply or an automated rejection. — reached an interview stage. One became a job — for four months, until the position was eliminated.

Public vs. private

Public sector organisations responded at a noticeably higher rate than private ones. This likely reflects the legal obligation Danish public bodies have to acknowledge applications, rather than a difference in interest.

What the data says

Silence is the norm. Nearly half of concluded applications received no reply, which says more about how hiring works here than about fit.

The funnel compresses fast. The drop-off between applying and being seen is steep, and almost entirely invisible to the applicant.

English-language applications to Danish companies had a slightly higher response rate than Danish-language ones. The likely explanation: English roles tend to go to larger organisations with more professionally managed HR teams.
